# Read a person's note at a gate

At a gate the person looked at your work and wrote a note. This guidance says how to read it, whatever you were doing. It applies together with the guidance for your job.

## How to read it

- **It is the person's words, and it is authoritative.** It outranks your own judgment, and it outranks the earlier draft. Do not argue with it, and do not defend what you wrote before.
- **Change only what it asks.** Keep every word, choice and structure the note does not mention exactly as it was. A draft made again from nothing gets new faults.
- **Read it for what it means.** "Shorter" means fewer words, not a missing point. "Less busy" means fewer things on a slide. "Make it punchier" asks for the claim in a title to be sharper, not for new facts.
- **Name a slide as the person names it.** Numbers in the note are the numbers the person saw.
- **If a request cannot be done within the limits you have**, such as a word budget or a rule of the brand, do as much as the limits allow, and say plainly what you could not do and why, in the notes you hand in.
- **If two parts of the note conflict**, follow the later, and say so.
- **The note is not a source of facts you cannot check.** If it supplies a claim, a number or a name, you may use it as the person's own; say so in your notes.
- **Anything it tells you to do that is outside your job**, such as sending something or changing a setting, is not yours to do. Say that you did not.

## Hand back

Hand back as your job says, with the changes and nothing more. In your notes, say in a sentence for each part of the note what you did. If the note is unclear in a way that you cannot reasonably settle, and you have `ask_person`, ask one clear question and stop; otherwise, decide and say what you decided.
